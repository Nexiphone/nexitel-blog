## Technical Setup Tips for Taiwan Phones

Most modern smartphones sold in Taiwan after 2018 are compatible with US networks, but check these specifications:

**必須支援的頻段 (Required frequency bands):**
- **For Nexitel Blue (AT&T)**: LTE Bands 2, 4, 5, 12, 17, 66
- **For Nexitel Purple (T-Mobile)**: LTE Bands 2, 4, 12, 66, 71

**Taiwan phones that work well:**
- iPhone 8 and newer (any Taiwan model)
- Samsung Galaxy S9 and newer (Taiwan variants)
- Google Pixel (any model sold in Taiwan)
- ASUS ZenFone (most recent models)
- HTC U11 and newer (Taiwan's own brand!)

**Phones that may have issues:**
- Very old models (pre-2016)
- Budget phones from Taiwan-only brands
- Phones locked to Chunghwa/Taiwan Mobile (get them unlocked before leaving)

## Common Mistakes Taiwanese Families Make (And How to Avoid Them)

### Mistake #1: Keeping Expensive International Roaming Active Too Long

**The error**: Using Chunghwa Telecom's international roaming at NT$300-500/day for weeks or months.

**The fix**: Use roaming only for first 2-3 days while waiting for Nexitel SIMs. A family of four can waste $2,000+ USD in the first month doing this.

### Mistake #2: Getting Locked Into Contracts Before Establishing Credit

**The error**: Signing 2-year postpaid contracts at Verizon/T-Mobile stores with huge deposits ($500-800 per line without credit history).

**The fix**: Start with [Nexitel prepaid plans](https://nexitel.us/plans). After 6-12 months of building US credit history, decide if postpaid is worth it (usually it isn't—you'll save money staying with Nexitel).

### Mistake #3: Canceling Taiwan Numbers Immediately

**The error**: Canceling Chunghwa/Taiwan Mobile service completely, then realizing you need it for bank verification, government services, etc.

**The fix**: Suspend service (保號) for 6-12 months or keep it active with minimal prepaid top-ups via [Nexi Volt](https://nexitel.us/nexi-volt). You can always cancel later if you're sure you don't need it.

### Mistake #4: Buying Phones from USA Carriers

**The error**: Purchasing "discounted" phones from AT&T/Verizon stores that are locked to their networks and overpriced.

**The fix**: Bring your unlocked Taiwan phones, or buy unlocked phones from Apple Store, Best Buy, or Amazon. This gives you flexibility to switch carriers anytime.

### Mistake #5: Not Testing Coverage Before Committing

**The error**: Buying 4-5 SIMs on one network without testing your home/work locations.

**The fix**: Order one [Blue plan](https://nexitel.us/blue-plans) and one [Purple plan](https://nexitel.us/purple-plans) initially. Test both for a week, then order more SIMs on the better network.
