## Why Keeping Your US Number Matters When You Move Abroad

Whether you're relocating for work, returning home after years in the States, or splitting time between countries, your US phone number connects you to bank accounts, employer systems, two-factor authentication codes, and family members who expect to reach you on a familiar line. Losing that number can lock you out of critical accounts and create weeks of administrative headaches.

The good news: you absolutely **can** keep your US number active while living abroad. The method depends on your carrier's policies, how you use the number, and whether you're willing to pay domestic or international rates.

## How Prepaid Plans Handle International Use

Most US carriers—postpaid and prepaid alike—treat prolonged international use differently. Here's what you need to know:

### Postpaid Carrier Restrictions

Major postpaid carriers (Verizon, AT&T, T-Mobile retail plans) typically allow **short-term** international roaming—a few weeks for vacation or business travel. If you stay abroad for 60–90 consecutive days, many will suspend your line or require you to upgrade to an expensive international plan. You're paying $50–$80/month for a service designed for US residents.

### Prepaid Carrier Flexibility

Prepaid carriers offer more flexibility because they don't enforce residency requirements the same way. As long as you keep the account funded and use the number periodically, you can maintain service. Two strategies work especially well:

**1. Wi-Fi Calling**  
Enable Wi-Fi calling on your device, and your phone behaves as if it's in the US—even when you're overseas. Calls and texts route over any Wi-Fi network (your apartment, a café, your office). Domestic calls are free, and you're not roaming.

**2. Periodic US Connections**  
Some travelers use a US SIM while visiting family once or twice a year, or keep the line active for banking and verification codes but don't use it daily. As long as the plan renews monthly, the number stays yours.

## How to Use Wi-Fi Calling While Living Abroad

Wi-Fi calling is the secret weapon for maintaining a US number overseas. Here's how to set it up:

### iPhone (iOS 15+)

1. Go to **Settings → Phone → Wi-Fi Calling**
2. Toggle **Wi-Fi Calling on This iPhone** to ON
3. Enter or confirm your emergency address (can be a US address)
4. Connect to any Wi-Fi network

Calls and texts now route over Wi-Fi. Your US number appears on caller ID as if you're calling from the States.

### Android (varies by manufacturer)

1. Go to **Settings → Network & Internet → Mobile Network**
2. Tap **Advanced → Wi-Fi Calling** (or search "Wi-Fi" in settings)
3. Enable Wi-Fi Calling
4. Enter emergency address if prompted

Some Android devices require you to enable Wi-Fi calling in the Phone app's settings instead.

### Important Notes

- **Airplane mode + Wi-Fi:** When you're abroad, enable airplane mode, then turn Wi-Fi back on. This forces all calls to route over Wi-Fi instead of expensive international roaming towers.
- **Outbound calls:** Calls to US numbers are treated as domestic (free on unlimited plans). Calls to local numbers in your host country may incur international rates—unless you use a separate local SIM for day-to-day calls.
- **Texts:** SMS and MMS work normally over Wi-Fi calling. Group texts, photos, and links all function as expected.

## Potential Pitfalls to Avoid

### Don't Let the Plan Lapse

If your payment method expires or the plan isn't renewed for 30–60 days, carriers may reclaim your number and assign it to someone else. Set up autopay and keep a valid credit/debit card on file.

### Check Wi-Fi Calling Compatibility

Older phones or certain international models may not support Wi-Fi calling, even if your plan includes it. Verify your device model supports the feature before relying on it exclusively.

### Understand Roaming vs. Wi-Fi Calling

If you accidentally leave Wi-Fi calling disabled and roaming enabled, your phone may connect to foreign towers at high per-minute rates. Always use airplane mode + Wi-Fi when abroad, or disable international roaming in your settings.

### Two-Factor Authentication Quirks

Some US banks and services send 2FA codes via SMS, which works fine over Wi-Fi calling. A few (uncommon) systems require the device to connect to a US cell tower, in which case you'd need to briefly disable airplane mode when visiting the US, or use an authenticator app instead.
