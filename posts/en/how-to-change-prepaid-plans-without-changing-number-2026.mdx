## Keep Your Number When Switching Prepaid Plans

Your phone number is tied to everything—your bank accounts, social media, work contacts, family connections back home, and authentication codes. Losing it when switching prepaid carriers feels like starting over. The good news? You don't have to.

In 2026, number portability is a federally protected right in the United States. You can switch from any prepaid carrier to another while keeping your existing number, whether you're moving from a major brand to a budget-friendly option or changing plans within the same network.

## Understanding Number Portability Rights

The FCC requires all wireless carriers to allow customers to port their numbers when switching providers. This applies to:

- Prepaid and postpaid plans
- Switching between different carriers
- Moving from one prepaid brand to another
- Changing from a major carrier to an MVNO (Mobile Virtual Network Operator)

Your current carrier cannot refuse your port request or charge excessive fees to release your number. Most carriers charge nothing for porting out, though you may need to settle any outstanding balance first.

## What You Need Before Porting

Before you start the porting process, gather this information from your current carrier:

1. **Account number** — This is different from your phone number. Check your online account, mobile app, or call customer service.
2. **PIN or password** — Your account security code. If you never set one, you'll need to create it before porting.
3. **Billing zip code** — The zip code associated with your account billing address.
4. **Account holder name** — Must match exactly across both carriers.

**Critical timing note:** Do not cancel your old service before starting the port. The port-out process automatically cancels your old line. If you cancel first, your number is released back to the carrier pool and becomes unrecoverable.

### Step 2: Sign Up Without Activating a New Number

When signing up with your new carrier:

- Select the option to "bring your own number" or "port a number"
- Do NOT choose "get a new number"
- Provide your account details from the current carrier
- Enter your porting information exactly as it appears on your old account

### Step 3: Submit Your Port Request

Your new carrier submits the port request to your old carrier. This triggers an automated process:

1. New carrier validates your account information
2. Old carrier receives and reviews the port request
3. If everything matches, the port is approved
4. Your number transfers to the new carrier
5. Your old account automatically closes

### Step 4: Wait for Completion

Port timing varies:

- **Within the same network family:** Often completes in minutes to hours
- **Between different networks:** Usually 2-4 hours, sometimes up to one business day
- **Landline to wireless:** Can take 3-7 business days

You'll receive confirmation via email or text when the port completes. Your old SIM card will stop working, and your new SIM will activate with your transferred number.

## Common Porting Problems and Solutions

### "Account Information Doesn't Match"

The most common rejection reason. Your account number, PIN, or name must match your old carrier's records exactly. Even spacing differences ("John Smith" vs "JohnSmith") can cause rejections.

**Fix:** Call your old carrier to verify your exact account number and PIN. Ask them to read back exactly how your name appears.

### Port Takes Longer Than Expected

Network congestion or validation delays can slow the process.

**Fix:** Contact your new carrier's support team after 24 hours. They can check the port status and identify any holds from your old carrier.

### Old Carrier Claims You Owe Money

Some carriers won't release numbers with outstanding balances.

**Fix:** Pay the balance or dispute it directly with the old carrier. Once cleared, restart the port request.

### You Accidentally Canceled First

If you canceled before porting, your number enters a brief grace period before permanent release.

**Fix:** Immediately contact your old carrier—within 24-48 hours. Some can reactivate your account temporarily to complete the port.

## Business Lines and Multi-Line Accounts

Porting business numbers or multiple lines follows the same process with added requirements:

- You may need to provide a business tax ID or authorization letter
- Each line needs individual porting information
- Business account holder must authorize all ports
- Some carriers offer bulk porting tools for 5+ lines

Verify that the account holder matches on both carriers. If lines are under your business name, they can't port to an individual account without additional documentation.

## What Happens to Your Old Account

When your port completes:

1. **Old account closes automatically** — No need to call and cancel
2. **Prorated refunds** — Some carriers refund unused prepaid time; others don't
3. **Final bill** — Postpaid accounts may receive a final bill for partial month usage
4. **SIM card** — Your old SIM becomes inactive and can be discarded

If you had auto-refill enabled, disable it before porting to avoid being charged for the next cycle right as you switch.

## Timing Your Switch Strategically

To minimize costs and avoid service gaps:

- **Port mid-cycle** if your old carrier doesn't refund unused time
- **Port after your renewal date** if your old carrier does offer prorated refunds
- **Port on a non-critical day** — Don't switch the day before an important appointment where you need reliable service
- **Port during business hours** — Support teams are available if issues arise

## Special Cases: MVNOs and Virtual Carriers

MVNOs (carriers that use major networks but operate independently) follow the same FCC porting rules. You can port:

- From an MVNO to a major carrier
- From a major carrier to an MVNO
- Between different MVNOs

Nexitel Blue runs on AT&T's network, and Nexitel Purple uses T-Mobile's network. Both support full number portability from any US carrier, including major brands and other MVNOs.

## Dual-SIM and eSIM Considerations

If your phone supports dual-SIM or eSIM:

- You can keep your old number active while testing a new carrier
- Port only when you're satisfied with the new service
- Use one SIM for domestic service, another for international calling via VoIP

eSIM devices can hold multiple carrier profiles, making it easier to maintain a backup line while transitioning.
