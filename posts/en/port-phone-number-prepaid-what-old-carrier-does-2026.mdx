## What Happens Behind the Scenes When You Port Out

You've decided to switch to a better prepaid plan. You initiate the port. Then you wait. But what's actually happening at your old carrier while your number transfers? Understanding this process helps you avoid surprise charges, recover unused balances, and time your switch perfectly.

## The Exact Moment Your Port Request Arrives

When your new carrier (like Nexitel) submits your port request, your old carrier receives an automated notification in their porting database. This happens within minutes, but the actual transfer doesn't occur immediately.

**What your old carrier does first:**

- Validates your account number and PIN/password
- Checks that the name and address match their records
- Confirms the number is eligible for porting (not suspended, not flagged)
- Queues the port for processing (usually 2-4 hours for wireless, up to 1 business day for landlines)

If anything doesn't match, they reject the port automatically. This is why having your exact account details ready is critical when you switch to [Nexitel Blue plans](https://www.nexitel.us/plans) starting at $10/month or [Nexitel Purple plans](https://www.nexitel.us/plans) from $5/month.

## Your Service Goes Dark: The Transfer Window

The actual port happens in a brief window—often just 5-15 minutes for prepaid carriers. During this time:

1. **Your old carrier deactivates your SIM** — calls drop, data stops, texts fail
2. **The number releases from their network** — it's in transit between carriers
3. **Your new carrier activates the number** — service resumes on the new network

For postpaid accounts, this often happens overnight. For prepaid, it can occur any time during business hours once approved.

**Common scenario for international students and workers:** You port from a major carrier to Nexitel Purple on a Friday afternoon. Your old SIM dies at 3 PM. Your Nexitel SIM activates by 3:20 PM. You're now on T-Mobile's network for $5/month instead of paying four times that amount.

## Account Closure: Automatic and Immediate

The moment your number successfully ports out, your old carrier's system automatically closes your account. There's no grace period, no "are you sure?" prompt. The port itself is your cancellation notice.

**What gets closed:**

- Your primary account (the one with the ported number)
- Any auto-pay arrangements linked to that account
- Loyalty rewards, accumulated points, or tenure benefits
- Access to the old carrier's app and online account portal (usually within 24-48 hours)

**What stays open temporarily:**

- Accounts with other lines (if you had a family plan and only ported one number)
- Pending support tickets (until resolved or 30 days, whichever comes first)
- Billing disputes or payment arrangements (these transfer to collections if unpaid)

If you had multiple lines and only ported one, the remaining lines stay active. But your plan structure may change—family plans often revert to single-line pricing, which can be more expensive.

## Final Bills for Postpaid Customers

Postpaid accounts generate one last bill, usually within 1-3 billing cycles after you port out.

**What appears on your final bill:**

- Prorated charges for the days you used service that month
- Any outstanding device payment balances (these don't disappear when you port)
- Early termination fees if you were under contract
- Taxes and regulatory fees for the partial month
- Credits for unused portions of paid-in-advance features

**Example:** You port out on the 15th of the month. Your billing cycle runs 1st-30th. You'll be charged for 15 days of service, plus any device payments, plus any ETFs. If you paid $50/month, expect roughly $25 plus fees on your final bill.

Device payment plans are legally separate from service agreements. Porting doesn't cancel them. You must continue paying off that phone, or pay the remaining balance in full.

## Refunds and Credits for Prepaid Customers

Prepaid is simpler but less forgiving. Most carriers have a no-refund policy for unused prepaid balances.

**Typical prepaid scenarios:**

- **You paid for 30 days, port on day 10:** You lose the remaining 20 days. No refund.
- **You had a $50 account balance:** Some carriers refund automatically, most don't. Check your terms.
- **You had unused international calling credit:** Almost never refunded unless required by state law.

**State-specific exceptions:** California, New York, and a few other states mandate refunds of prepaid balances over a certain threshold (often $5-$10) if you request them within 30 days. Check your old carrier's terms and your state's consumer protection laws.

## Timing Your Port to Minimize Losses

Strategic timing can save you money, especially on prepaid accounts.

**Best practices:**

1. **Port on the last day of your billing cycle** — maximize the value of what you've already paid
2. **Use up any account balance first** — make those international calls, send those texts, use that data before you port
3. **Check for auto-renewal dates** — if your plan renews on the 1st and you port on the 2nd, you've wasted a full month's payment
4. **Request refunds immediately** — don't wait; some carriers only process refund requests within 15-30 days of account closure

**For visa holders and temporary residents:** If you're on an F-1 student visa or H1B work visa and switching between carriers frequently (moving between internships, changing cities for work), timing your ports around travel can double your savings. ## What Happens to Your Physical SIM Card

Your old SIM becomes a useless piece of plastic the moment the port completes. The carrier deactivates it remotely.

**Can you reuse it?** No. Even if you wanted to reactivate service with your old carrier later, they'd issue a new SIM.

**Should you destroy it?** Not necessary for security—it's already deactivated. But if you're concerned about personal data, cut through the chip.

**What about eSIM?** Same story. Your old eSIM profile deactivates automatically. You'll download a new eSIM profile from your new carrier. With Nexitel, you can activate on eSIM or physical SIM for all [Blue plans](https://www.nexitel.us/plans) and [Purple plans](https://www.nexitel.us/plans).

## Your Data and Account History

Account closure doesn't mean your data disappears immediately.

**What carriers typically retain:**

- Call and text logs (6 months to 7 years, depending on carrier and regulations)
- Payment history (7 years for tax and legal purposes)
- Personal information (name, address, SSN/ITIN) for regulatory compliance
- Customer service interaction records (1-3 years)

**What you lose access to:**

- Downloadable call detail records after 30-90 days
- Saved voicemails (most carriers delete these within 30 days of account closure)
- Text message archives stored in the carrier's cloud

**Important for visa documentation:** If you're on an H1B visa or applying for a green card and need proof of US residency or phone activity for immigration purposes, download your records BEFORE you port. Once your account closes, getting historical records becomes a bureaucratic nightmare.

## Auto-Pay and Linked Services

Your old carrier's auto-pay stops automatically when the account closes, but linked third-party services don't know that.

**Common issues:**

- **Banking alerts** — your bank may flag the missed auto-pay as unusual activity
- **Two-factor authentication** — services using SMS codes to your old number will fail until you update them
- **Streaming services** — if you bundled services (like Hulu or Apple Music) with your phone plan, those may cancel too

## Business Accounts and Tax Implications

Business accounts face additional complications when porting.

**What your old carrier does:**

- Sends final bill to the business billing address on record
- Reports charges to the business tax ID (EIN) for that tax year
- May require a business account closure form (in addition to the port)
- Cancels any associated services (toll-free numbers, conference bridges, etc.)

**Tax considerations:** If you've been deducting phone service as a business expense, your final bill and any ETFs are still deductible in the year paid. Keep documentation.

## Credit Checks and Your Credit Report

Porting out of a postpaid account can affect your credit, but usually doesn't.

**When it matters:**

- If you leave an unpaid balance, your old carrier may send it to collections (negative mark on credit)
- If you had a device payment plan and stop paying, that's reported as a defaulted loan (major negative mark)
- If you paid on time and close in good standing, there's no impact

**When it doesn't matter:**

- Prepaid accounts don't report to credit bureaus (no impact either way)
- Successfully porting your number is not a negative event
- Closing a phone account doesn't hurt your credit score the way closing a credit card might

For recent immigrants, international students, or anyone building US credit history, switching to prepaid (like Nexitel) means one less account that could potentially damage your credit if you miss a payment during a visa transition or travel period.

## Getting Your Final Documents

Your old carrier is legally required to provide certain documents if you request them.

**What you can request:**

- Final bill (usually available online for 90 days after account closure)
- Payment history (for tax or dispute purposes)
- Call detail records (while still available)
- Porting history and confirmation

**How to request:** Most carriers allow you to download these from your account portal while it's still accessible. After portal access closes, you'll need to contact customer service (often a painful process once you're no longer a customer).

**Pro tip:** Download everything before you port. Take screenshots of your account summary, billing history, and any promotional promises. Once you're gone, getting documentation becomes exponentially harder.

## The 24-Hour Window: When Things Go Wrong

Occasionally, ports fail mid-process. When that happens, you're in limbo.

**What your old carrier does:**

- If the port fails before completion, they reactivate your service (usually within 1-24 hours)
- If it fails after completion, they can't help—you need to work with your new carrier
- If there's a dispute (wrong number ported, account info mismatch), both carriers freeze the number until resolved

**Most common port failures:**

1. Account number or PIN was incorrect
2. Name on the account doesn't match exactly (middle initials matter!)
3. The number was suspended or had a porting freeze
4. Your old carrier rejected it due to an unpaid balance
