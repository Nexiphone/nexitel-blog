## Understanding Mid-Month Network Switches at Nexitel

You've been using Nexitel Blue on the AT&T network, but now you're wondering if T-Mobile's coverage might work better for you. Maybe your workplace has better T-Mobile signal, or you've moved to a new neighborhood where Purple performs better. The good news? Yes, you can switch from Blue to Purple mid-month—but there are important details to understand before you make the move.

This guide walks you through exactly what happens when you switch networks, how your billing works, and what steps you need to take to make the transition smooth.

## How Nexitel Blue and Purple Plans Work

Before diving into the switching process, let's clarify what you're working with:

**Nexitel Blue Plans** run on the AT&T network and include:
- BlueDom: $10/month
- BlueTalk Global: $12/month
- BlueConnect: $15/month
- BluePremium: $25/month
- BluePro: $35/month

**Nexitel Purple Plans** run on the T-Mobile network and include:
- PurpleStart: $5/month
- PurpleText: $9/month
- PurpleData: $12/month
- PurpleMax: $15/month

All Nexitel plans are prepaid, meaning you pay upfront for service. This prepaid structure is what makes mid-month switching possible, though it requires careful planning.

## What Happens to Your Current Service When You Switch

When you decide to switch from Blue to Purple mid-month, here's the sequence of events:

### Your Blue Plan Continues Until Expiration

Your current Blue plan will remain active until the end of your paid billing cycle. If you paid for BlueDom on October 1st, your service runs through October 31st regardless of when you activate a Purple plan.

### No Automatic Cancellation

Activating a Purple plan does not automatically cancel your Blue plan. These are treated as separate line activations in the system. If you want to stop using your Blue line entirely, you'll need to let it expire naturally by not renewing it.

### Your Phone Number Options

You have two paths forward:

**Option 1: Keep Your Blue Number Active**
- Activate Purple on a new SIM with a new number
- Both lines remain active until you choose to discontinue one
- Useful if you want to test Purple coverage before fully committing

**Option 2: Port Your Blue Number to Purple**
- Request a number port from Blue to Purple
- Your Blue service ends when the port completes
- You'll lose any remaining days on your Blue plan
- Purple service begins immediately after the port

## The Billing Reality: No Prorated Refunds

Here's the crucial part many customers ask about: **Nexitel does not offer prorated refunds or credits when you switch mid-month.**

If you paid for a full month of BlueDom ($10) on October 1st and switch to Purple on October 15th, you will not receive a refund or credit for the unused two weeks of Blue service. This is standard for prepaid carriers—you're paying for a full month of access, and that access remains available whether you use it or not.

### Why No Prorating?

Prepaid plans work differently from postpaid contracts. When you pay for October service, you're securing network access for the entire month. The infrastructure cost is the same whether you actively use the service for 30 days or just 5. This is why prepaid plans are significantly cheaper than postpaid—there's less administrative flexibility, but the trade-off is lower prices.

## Smart Strategies to Minimize Wasted Service

Since you can't get refunds, timing your switch strategically saves money:

### Strategy 1: Switch at the End of Your Cycle

Wait until the last few days of your Blue billing cycle before activating Purple. If your Blue plan expires on October 31st, activate Purple on October 28th or 29th. You'll overlap by just a few days instead of losing weeks of service.

### Strategy 2: Test Purple with a New Number First

Instead of immediately porting your number, activate a Purple plan with a temporary number. Test coverage in your home, workplace, and frequently visited areas. If Purple works well, port your number when your Blue plan expires. If not, stick with Blue without having wasted money.

### Strategy 3: Use Your Blue Days for International Top-Ups

If you're switching mid-month and have Blue days remaining, use the Nexi Volt service to send mobile recharge credit to family overseas. Whether they use Jio, China Mobile, Vodafone Egypt, or other carriers, you can make productive use of your remaining service time.

## Step-by-Step: How to Switch from Blue to Purple

### Step 1: Check Your Blue Expiration Date

Log into your Nexitel account and verify when your current Blue plan expires. This determines your optimal switching timeline.

### Step 2: Choose Your Purple Plan

Visit [https://www.nexitel.us/plans](https://www.nexitel.us/plans) and select the Purple plan that matches your needs. Compare data allowances, talk time, and international features carefully.

### Step 3: Order Your Purple SIM

Order a Purple SIM kit if you don't already have one. Many customers keep both Blue and Purple SIMs on hand for exactly this kind of flexibility.

### Step 4: Decide on Number Porting

**If keeping your number:**
- Wait until your Blue plan expires
- Activate Purple with your existing number
- No service gap occurs

**If getting a new number:**
- Activate Purple immediately
- Use both lines during overlap period
- Let Blue expire naturally

### Step 5: Activate Your Purple Service

Follow the activation instructions included with your Purple SIM. The process takes just a few minutes and can be completed entirely online.

### Step 6: Test Your Purple Service

Make test calls, send texts, and verify data works correctly in your usual locations before fully relying on the Purple line.

## Special Considerations for Different Use Cases

### For International Calling Needs

If you're currently using BlueTalk Global ($12/month) for international calls, switching to Purple doesn't mean losing that capability. Consider pairing any Purple plan with [NexiTalk](https://www.nexitel.us/nexitalk), our VoIP international calling service. This gives you flexibility to call family in India, China, Taiwan, Egypt, UAE, and dozens of other countries without being tied to a specific cellular plan.

### For Travelers and Visitors

If you're in the US temporarily—perhaps on an F-1 visa, H1B work authorization, or visiting family—switching networks mid-month might not be worth the complexity. Instead, test both networks by activating a second line briefly, then choose one network for your next monthly renewal.

### For Business Lines

Some customers run business operations on Nexitel and need consistent uptime. If you're managing customer communications, never port your primary business number mid-month. Instead, activate Purple with a new number, redirect customers gradually, then let Blue expire naturally.

## Coverage Differences: What Drives People to Switch

Understanding why customers switch from Blue to Purple helps you make an informed decision:

### AT&T Network Strengths (Blue)

- Often superior in rural areas and smaller cities
- Strong building penetration in many regions
- Reliable in suburbs and along major highways

### T-Mobile Network Strengths (Purple)

- Expanding 5G coverage in urban centers
- Competitive speeds in metropolitan areas
- Often better indoor coverage in newer buildings

Your specific location matters far more than general network reputation. A network that works perfectly for someone across town might underperform at your home or workplace.

## What If You Want to Switch Back?

The same rules apply in reverse. If you switch from Purple to Blue and change your mind, you can switch back—but again, no prorated refunds. This reinforces why testing with a temporary number first is valuable.

Some customers actually maintain both Blue and Purple lines long-term, using whichever has better signal in their current location. With Purple plans starting at just $5/month and Blue plans from $10/month, dual-line flexibility is surprisingly affordable.

## Making Your Decision: Is Mid-Month Switching Worth It?

Ask yourself these questions:

**How many days remain in my Blue cycle?**
If fewer than five days, switching now makes sense. If more than two weeks, waiting saves money.

**Is my Blue service actually problematic?**
Occasional dropped calls might not justify immediate action. Consistent no-service areas at home or work do.

**Can I test Purple without porting?**
If yes, testing first is always smarter than committing your primary number immediately.

**Do I need international calling?**
Your international needs might be better served by keeping your current plan and adding NexiTalk rather than switching networks.
