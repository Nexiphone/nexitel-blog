## Running Two Networks on One Phone: Blue + Purple Dual SIM

Dual SIM phones let you run two lines on one device. If you're using Nexitel, that means you can activate both a Blue plan (AT&T network) and a Purple plan (T-Mobile network) on the same handset—giving you coverage on both major US carriers at once.

This setup is popular with people who need maximum reliability: if one network is down or slow, the other picks up the slack. You get two US phone numbers, texts on both lines, and the ability to choose which network handles each call or app.

Here's how to set up Blue and Purple together, which phones support it, and why running both networks makes sense.

## Why Run Blue and Purple at the Same Time?

### Network Redundancy

AT&T (Blue) and T-Mobile (Purple) have different tower locations. A building with weak AT&T signal might have strong T-Mobile, and vice versa. Running both means you always have a fallback.

### Two US Numbers for Different Purposes

One number for work, one for personal. One for US contacts, one for international calls. One for Wi-Fi Calling home to family, one for local texts and deliveries. Two lines let you keep contexts separate.

### Price Flexibility

Nexitel Blue plans start at $10/month ([BlueDom](https://www.nexitel.us/plans)). Purple plans start at $5/month ([PurpleStart](https://www.nexitel.us/plans)). You can mix and match based on what each line needs—run a full-featured Blue plan for your main number and a low-cost Purple backup, or vice versa.

### Coverage While Traveling Across the US

Some rural areas have strong AT&T but weak T-Mobile. Others are the opposite. With both networks active, you're covered in more places without switching SIMs or carriers.

## Which Phones Support Dual SIM with eSIM + Physical SIM?

Most modern smartphones support dual SIM in one of two ways:

### eSIM + Physical SIM (Most Common)

- **iPhone XS and newer** (XS, XS Max, XR, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16 series)
- **Google Pixel 3 and newer** (Pixel 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 series)
- **Samsung Galaxy S20 and newer** (S20, S21, S22, S23, S24, Note20, Z Flip/Fold series)
- **Many Motorola, OnePlus, and other Android flagships** released after 2020

These phones have one physical SIM slot and one eSIM slot. You can activate one Nexitel plan as eSIM and use the physical slot for the other.

### Dual eSIM (iPhone 13 and Newer in the US)

iPhone models sold in the US since the iPhone 14 have **no physical SIM slot**—only dual eSIM. You can run two eSIM profiles at once. Both your Blue and Purple plans can be eSIM.

When you [buy a plan online at Nexitel](https://www.nexitel.us/activate), you receive an eSIM by email. You can activate two separate eSIMs (one Blue, one Purple) on the same iPhone 14 or newer.

Once both lines are active, go to Settings > Cellular (iOS) or Settings > Network & Internet > SIMs (Android).

You can enable both lines for iMessage, or choose one. Both numbers will ring for FaceTime calls.

**Contact-Specific Lines:**
Assign specific contacts to always use one line. For example, set your work contacts to use Blue and family to use Purple.

## Important Notes About Dual SIM with Nexitel

### You Get Two Different US Phone Numbers

Each activation—Blue or Purple—issues a **new US number**. You cannot port an existing number into Nexitel, and you cannot move a number between Blue and Purple. If you activate both, you will have two separate phone numbers.

### Blue and Purple Are Separate Lines

Your Blue plan and Purple plan are independent. Each has its own 30-day cycle. You [renew each plan separately](https://www.nexitel.us/recharge) every month. There is no auto-renew or autopay—you manually recharge each line when it's due.

### No Roaming on Either Network

Neither Blue nor Purple plans include international roaming. If you travel outside the US, neither line will work on foreign towers. However, plans with **Wi-Fi Calling** (BlueTalk Global, BluePremium, BluePro) let you call and text US numbers over Wi-Fi from anywhere in the world—useful for staying in touch with US contacts or family back home while traveling.

### Data Is Not Shared Between Lines

If your Blue plan has 10GB and Purple has 6GB, you have 16GB total—but they're in separate buckets. You choose one line as your "data default" in settings. To switch, you manually change the default. Some phones let you set per-app data preferences (use Blue for maps, Purple for social media).

### Both Lines Can Receive Calls and Texts Simultaneously

When someone calls your Blue number, that line rings. When someone texts your Purple number, you get the message. Both lines are active at the same time. You can receive on both, but you can only make one call at a time (the phone picks the line you've set for outgoing calls, or asks you each time).

## Troubleshooting Dual SIM Setup

**Problem: Second eSIM won't activate**
- Make sure your phone supports dual eSIM (iPhone 13 US model and newer do; some older phones only support one eSIM at a time even if they have the feature)
- Restart the phone and try adding the eSIM again
- Check that you're scanning the correct QR code for the second plan

**Problem: Can't tell which line is ringing**
- Go to Settings > Cellular > [line name] and assign custom ringtones or vibration patterns to each line

**Problem: Data isn't working on one line**
- Check Settings > Cellular > Cellular Data—make sure the correct line is selected as the data default
- Some phones require you to enable "Allow Cellular Data Switching" to use the non-default line's data when the default has no signal

**Problem: Only one line works at a time**
- Confirm both lines show as "Active" in Settings > Cellular
- Make sure neither SIM is disabled or in airplane mode
- Restart the phone

**Problem: Calls go out on the wrong line**
- Go to Settings > Cellular > Default Voice Line and choose the line you want for outgoing calls
- Or enable "Ask Each Time" so the phone prompts you before dialing
