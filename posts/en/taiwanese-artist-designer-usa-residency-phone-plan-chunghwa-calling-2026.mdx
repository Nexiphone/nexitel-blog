## Why Taiwanese Creatives Need a Prepaid Plan (Not a Contract)

Most USA artist residencies last 4–12 weeks. Design fellowships might run 3–6 months. Traditional postpaid plans from AT&T, Verizon, or T-Mobile require:

- **Social Security Number (SSN)** — which you may not have yet on a J-1 visa
- **Credit checks** — often denied for international visitors with no US credit history
- **Long-term contracts** — 12–24 months, with early termination fees of $200–$350 USD
- **High monthly fees** — $50–$80 USD/month for basic plans

**Prepaid plans solve all of this.** No SSN required, no credit check, no contract. Pay monthly (or even weekly), and cancel anytime—perfect when your residency ends and you're flying back to Taoyuan International Airport.

## Tips for Taiwanese Artists Managing USA + Taiwan Connectivity

### 1. Use WiFi Calling for Taiwan Numbers
Most residencies have WiFi. Enable WiFi calling on your unlocked phone, and you can receive calls to your Taiwan number (if you keep it active) without international roaming charges.

### 2. Keep Your Taiwan Number Active (or Pause It)
If you have a 中華電信 or Taiwan Mobile postpaid plan, ask about "suspension" (停話) services for 3–6 months. This keeps your number without paying full monthly fees. When you return to Taiwan, reactivate it.

If you have prepaid, use Nexi Volt to top it up remotely every 30–60 days to keep the number alive.

### 3. Use Google Voice or WhatsApp for Free Taiwan Calls (But Not Always Reliable)
Free services like WhatsApp, LINE, or Google Voice work well—but require your family to have smartphones and data. If your grandparents in Chiayi only have a 中華電信 landline, NexiTalk is the reliable solution.

### 4. Budget for Phone Costs in Your Residency Stipend
Most residencies provide $500–$2000 USD stipends. Budget $25–$40/month for mobile service, and you'll stay connected without financial stress.
