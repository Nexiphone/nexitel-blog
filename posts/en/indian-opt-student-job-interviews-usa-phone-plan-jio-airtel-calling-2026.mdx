Here's the reality: recruiters from Amazon, Google, Microsoft, or startups in Silicon Valley will call you at random hours. Indian students on Optional Practical Training need a US phone number that **actually works** — not just for job interviews, but for two-factor authentication, apartment applications, SSN appointments, and staying in touch with family back in Mumbai, Delhi, or Bangalore. ## The OPT Student's Communication Challenge

Between May and September each year, over 100,000 Indian students transition from F-1 to OPT status. During this critical 12-24 month window, you're juggling:

- **Multiple recruiter calls daily** — often from blocked numbers or area codes you don't recognize
- **Video interviews on Zoom/Teams** — requiring stable data when you're commuting or at a library
- **Family anxiety back home** — parents calling on Jio or Airtel wanting updates on your job search
- **Authentication codes** — every banking app, credit card, apartment portal needs SMS verification
- **Budget constraints** — you're not earning yet, or just started a contract role paying bi-weekly

Most international students make one of two mistakes: they either overpay for a big carrier "unlimited" plan at $50-70/month, or they go too cheap with a sketchy MVNO that drops calls during the interview with your hiring manager.
