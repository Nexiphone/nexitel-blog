## Understanding the Port-Out Process: What Your Old Carrier Does

Porting your number to a new carrier feels like magic—one moment you're on AT&T or T-Mobile, the next you're activating service with someone new. But what actually happens to your old account when you complete that port? Many people switching to prepaid carriers worry about double billing, mysterious charges, or whether they need to manually cancel. Let's clear up the confusion.

## Your Old Account Cancels Automatically

Here's the most important thing to know: **when you successfully port your number out, your old carrier account terminates automatically**. You don't need to call customer service, visit a store, or submit a cancellation form. The port-out itself is the cancellation.

This happens because your phone number is tied to your account as the unique identifier. When the number moves to the new carrier, the Federal Communications Commission regulations require the old carrier to release both the number and close the associated account. It's not optional for them—it's mandated.

### The Timeline

- **Port initiation**: You provide your account number and PIN to your new carrier
- **Port processing**: Usually 2-24 hours for wireless-to-wireless ports
- **Port completion**: The moment your new SIM activates with your old number
- **Old account closure**: Happens simultaneously with port completion

## What About Charges After You Port?

This is where things get tricky, and where most port-out confusion happens.

### Postpaid Carriers (Verizon, AT&T, T-Mobile contract plans)

If you're leaving a postpaid carrier, you're typically billed for the full month regardless of when you port out. Postpaid billing is always for the upcoming month or current billing cycle. Here's what to expect:

- **No prorated refunds**: If you port out on day 5 of a 30-day cycle, you've already been charged for the full 30 days
- **Final bill arrival**: Expect one last bill covering your service through the port date, plus any device payments, late fees, or overage charges
- **Early termination fees**: If you're under contract, these apply and will appear on your final bill
- **Device payment plans**: These don't disappear—you still owe the remaining device balance

### Prepaid Carriers (Metro, Cricket, Boost, Visible)

Prepaid services work differently because you pay before using service:

- **No refunds for unused time**: If you had 20 days left when you ported, that money is gone
- **Most carriers keep the balance**: Cricket, Metro, and Boost typically don't refund unused service periods
- **Read the terms**: Some smaller MVNOs offer prorated refunds, but it's rare
- **Top-up balances**: Unused airtime or data-add-on balances are forfeited

## Can You Get Your Money Back?

Generally, no—but there are exceptions:

### Situations Where Refunds Might Apply

**Security deposits**: If you paid a deposit to a postpaid carrier and maintained good payment history, you should receive this back after your final bill is settled (usually 60-90 days).

**Billing errors**: If your old carrier overcharged you or billed for services you didn't authorize, dispute these charges even after porting.

**Device trade-in credits**: If you were receiving monthly credits for a device trade-in, porting out forfeits the remaining credits. You'll need to pay the full remaining device balance.

### What You Won't Get Back

- Unused prepaid service time
- Activation fees or SIM card fees
- Monthly service fees for postpaid (you used the service for that billing cycle)
- International add-on packs (even if unused)

## Common Port-Out Mistakes That Cost Money

### Mistake 1: Not Timing Your Port Strategically

If you're on prepaid, porting out the day after your plan renews means you just paid for 30 days you won't use. Instead, port out just before your renewal date to maximize the service you've already paid for.

For postpaid, you're charged the same amount regardless, but porting mid-cycle means getting a final bill that covers partial service—which can be confusing.

### Mistake 2: Forgetting About Linked Services

Many people have services tied to their mobile account:

- **Insurance plans**: These cancel when you port, and you lose coverage immediately
- **International calling add-ons**: Vanish with the port
- **Family plan discounts**: Other lines on your account may see price increases when you leave
- **Bundled home internet**: Some carriers offer mobile+home bundles with discounts

### Mistake 3: Not Capturing Account Details First

Before you port, document everything:

- Download all bills and payment history
- Screenshot your plan details and add-ons
- Save any international top-up receipts (important if you regularly used services like Nexi Volt to recharge phones back home)
- Note any promotional credits or agreements

### Mistake 4: Porting Too Early in Device Payments

If you owe $600 on a device and have been paying $25/month in credits, porting out means those credits stop immediately. You'll get a final bill for the full $600 balance. Many people don't realize this until the bill arrives.

## What Happens to Carrier-Specific Features?

### Visual Voicemail

Most carrier-provided visual voicemail services stop working immediately when you port. Your new carrier will provide their own version, but you'll lose access to old voicemails stored on your previous carrier's servers.

### Wi-Fi Calling

If you relied on Wi-Fi calling with your old carrier, that service terminates with the port. ## The Right Way to Port Out

### Step 1: Gather Information (Do This First)

Contact your current carrier or check your account online for:

- Account number (not your phone number—this is a separate identifier)
- Account PIN or password
- Billing address on file
- Any outstanding device balances

### Step 2: Choose Your Port Timing

- **Prepaid users**: Port within the last few days of your service period
- **Postpaid users**: Timing matters less, but mid-cycle means potentially confusing final bills
- **Contract users**: Calculate early termination fees and decide if waiting is worth it

### Step 3: Initiate Port with New Carrier

Provide the information from Step 1 to your new carrier. With Nexitel, this happens during activation—you'll enter your old carrier details, and the port begins automatically.

### Step 4: Don't Cancel Your Old Service

This is critical: **do not call your old carrier to cancel before porting**. If you cancel first, you lose your number. The port itself is the cancellation.

### Step 5: Keep Your Old SIM Active

Leave your old SIM in your phone until the port completes. You'll know it's done when your old SIM stops working and your new SIM connects to service with your existing number.

## What If Something Goes Wrong?

### Port Rejected

Common reasons:

- **Mismatched account information**: Name, address, or account number doesn't match carrier records
- **Outstanding balance**: Some carriers block ports until you pay what you owe
- **Account PIN incorrect**: Double-check this—it's different from your online password
- **Number not eligible**: Recently activated numbers may have a waiting period

### Partial Port Issues

Sometimes the port completes but:

- **Texts not working**: Usually resolves within 24 hours as routing updates
- **Data not connecting**: Check APN settings on your new carrier
- **Voicemail missing**: This is normal—old carrier voicemail is gone forever

### Double Billing Concerns

If you see charges from both your old and new carrier in the same month:

- **Postpaid**: You're seeing your final bill from the old carrier plus your first bill from the new carrier—this is normal
- **Prepaid**: You shouldn't be charged by the old carrier after porting, but you did already pay for the current service period (which you forfeit)
