## The Hidden Process Behind Number Porting

You've decided to switch to prepaid. You fill out a porting form, submit your account number and PIN, then… wait. But what's actually happening during those hours (or days) while your number transfers? Understanding the behind-the-scenes process helps you avoid service gaps, surprise bills, and the panic of a suddenly dead phone.

Whether you're an international student on an F-1 visa trying to save money, an H1B worker tired of overpaying, or anyone looking to switch to [Nexitel's prepaid plans](https://www.nexitel.us/plans), here's exactly what occurs when you port your number.

## The Automated Dance Between Carriers

### Step 1: Port Request Submission (Minute 0)

When you submit a port request to Nexitel—whether for a [Nexitel Blue plan](https://www.nexitel.us/plans) on AT&T's network or a [Nexitel Purple plan](https://www.nexitel.us/plans) on T-Mobile's network—your new carrier sends an electronic request to your old carrier through a centralized database system.

This request includes:
- Your phone number
- Account number with old carrier
- Account PIN or password
- Billing name and address
- Sometimes the last four digits of your SSN or Tax ID

**Critical detail**: Your information must match EXACTLY what your old carrier has on file. A middle initial mismatch or old address can trigger an automatic rejection.

### Step 2: Old Carrier Validation (Minutes 5-30)

Your old carrier's system automatically checks the port request against your account records. They're verifying:

- Is this account number valid and active?
- Does the PIN match?
- Does the name match?
- Is there an outstanding balance?
- Are there any port restrictions (like device payment plans)?

**What most people don't realize**: Your old carrier has financial incentive to delay or reject, but FCC regulations require them to process legitimate requests. Automated systems handle most checks, but complex accounts may trigger manual review.

### Step 3: Port Authorization (30 minutes - 4 hours)

Once validated, your old carrier sends back a "port authorized" message with a FOC (Firm Order Commitment) date and time—the exact moment your number will transfer.

During this window, both carriers are preparing:

**Your old carrier**:
- Flags your account for disconnection
- Calculates final bill (prorated if postpaid)
- Prepares to release your number from their database

**Your new carrier (Nexitel)**:
- Reserves your number in their system
- Prepares your SIM card activation
- Sets up your chosen plan configuration

### Step 4: The Actual Port (Usually 2-24 hours total)

At the FOC time, an automated process executes:

1. Your old carrier releases your number from their network
2. National number database updates (milliseconds)
3. Nexitel claims the number and activates your SIM
4. Routing tables update across carrier networks (5-15 minutes)
5. Your old SIM stops working
6. Your new Nexitel SIM becomes active

**The service gap myth**: Most ports complete with zero downtime. The database update happens so fast that calls and texts route correctly during the transition. However, if you're porting during peak hours or your old carrier uses legacy systems, you might experience 5-30 minutes of "Number not in service" messages.

## What Happens to Your Old Account

### Postpaid Accounts (Most Major Carriers)

Your account doesn't just disappear. Here's the sequence:

**Day 0 (Port completion)**:
- Service immediately terminates
- Device payment plans (if any) continue—you still owe the phone
- Your final billing cycle begins

**Days 1-7**:
- Final bill calculation includes prorated service charges
- Early termination fees apply if you're under contract
- Any device unlock requests are processed
- Autopay stops (usually)

**Days 7-30**:
- Final bill issued (typically prorated to port date)
- Previous month's charges included
- Possible "service through end of billing cycle" charges (carrier-dependent)

**What catches people off guard**: Some carriers charge through the end of your billing cycle even if you port mid-cycle. Others prorate. Read your old carrier's terms—T-Mobile and AT&T typically prorate, Verizon has varied policies depending on your specific plan.

### Prepaid Accounts (Old Prepaid Service)

Much simpler:
- Service stops immediately at port completion
- Any remaining balance or days are forfeited (no refunds)
- Auto-refill cancels automatically
- Account enters inactive status, eventually purged

This is why timing matters. If you just refilled your old prepaid plan with thirty days of service and port after three days, those remaining twenty-seven days are lost.

## The Technical Details Most Guides Skip

### Number Database Propagation

Your number doesn't just switch from Carrier A to Nexitel instantly across all systems. The Local Number Portability (LNP) database updates in stages:

1. **National database** (instant)
2. **Major carrier routing tables** (5-15 minutes)
3. **Smaller carrier and VoIP systems** (up to 48 hours)
4. **International carrier databases** (can take weeks)

**Real-world impact**: If you're an H1B worker who regularly receives calls from India, or an international student whose family uses China Mobile or China Unicom to call you, their carrier might still route to your old number for 24-48 hours after porting. The calls will fail or reach a "disconnected" message until their system updates.

For international calling in the other direction, [NexiTalk VoIP](https://www.nexitel.us/nexitalk) works immediately because it routes through updated US databases.

### Your SIM Card Status

**Old SIM**: The moment the port completes, your old SIM becomes a worthless piece of plastic. The carrier remotely deactivates the IMSI (International Mobile Subscriber Identity) and ICCID (SIM card number) in their system. Even if you reinsert it, it will show "No Service" or "SIM Not Provisioned."

**New Nexitel SIM**: During the port process, your new SIM is in a "pending" state. It's activated for limited functions but not fully live. The moment the port completes:
- Full network access activates
- Your phone registers on AT&T (Nexitel Blue) or T-Mobile (Nexitel Purple)
- Data, calling, and texting immediately function

The delay? Smaller carriers often require manual review, especially outside business hours.

### Scenario 3: Port Rejection and Retry

**What happens behind the scenes when ports fail**:

Your first attempt fails because your account number was wrong. Here's the actual sequence:

1. Nexitel submits request with incorrect account number
2. Old carrier's system auto-rejects (usually within 30 minutes)
3. Nexitel receives rejection code (specific reason like "Account number mismatch")
4. Your port status shows "Failed" or "Rejected"
5. You provide corrected information
6. Entire process starts over from scratch

**Each rejection adds 2-24 hours to your timeline.** This is why having your account number, PIN, and billing address perfectly accurate before starting matters enormously.

## What Your Old Carrier Does With Your Data

### Immediate Actions

- Call history: Archived but no longer accessible to you through their app/website
- Text messages: If stored on their servers (like Verizon Messages), they become inaccessible within hours
- Voicemail: Your voicemail box deactivates; messages are typically deleted within 24-48 hours
- Cloud backups: Carrier-specific cloud services (like Verizon Cloud) may remain accessible briefly if you created a separate account, but phone service integration ends

**Critical**: Download any texts, voicemails, or call logs you need BEFORE porting. Once the port completes, that data is effectively gone.

### Account Records Retention

Your old carrier keeps billing and account records for regulatory compliance (typically seven years), but you lose self-service access:

- Online account portal: Login usually disabled within 24-48 hours
- App access: Stops working immediately
- Customer service: Can still access your records if you call about final bills

### Financial Obligations

**What doesn't go away**:
- Device payment plans continue on their original schedule
- Early termination fees are billed immediately
- Outstanding balances follow normal collections processes
- Your credit report reflects any unpaid amounts

Porting doesn't erase debt. If you owe money for a phone or had service charges, expect those bills to follow you.

## Service Gaps: The Truth About Downtime

### Normal Port (95% of cases)

- Phone calls: 0-5 minutes of potential "number not in service" messages
- Text messages: Usually queue and deliver once port completes; might see 5-15 minute delay
- Data: Immediate switch once SIM activates on new network
- Voicemail: New voicemail system active immediately

### Problematic Port (5% of cases)

Issues that cause longer gaps:

1. **Weekend ports**: Some carriers have limited weekend support; Monday morning is when issues get resolved
2. **Port to different network technology**: Rare now, but porting between incompatible systems adds complexity
3. **Number routing errors**: Wrong carrier notified, database glitches; can cause 12-48 hour gaps
4. **International callback issues**: Your number might work for US calls but fail for international callers for days

**For international students and visa workers**: If you regularly receive calls from family using carriers like Jio, Airtel, Vi, China Mobile, or Chunghwa Telecom, warn them you might be unreachable for up to 48 hours during the port. Alternatively, set up [NexiTalk VoIP](https://www.nexitel.us/nexitalk) before porting so they can reach you on a stable VoIP number while your mobile number transitions.

## The Final Bill Surprise

Most port-related complaints center on unexpected final bills. Here's what triggers them:

### Prorated Charges

If you port on October 12th and your old billing cycle runs October 1-31, you're typically charged for:
- Full service October 1-12
- Any overages through October 12
- Previous month's charges if they bill a month behind

**Example**: Your September bill might not have been issued yet when you port in October. Your final bill includes both September's full charges AND October 1-12 prorated charges.

### Non-Prorated Charges

Some plans or carriers charge for the full month regardless of when you port:
- Certain business plans
- Some unlimited plans with specific contract terms
- Add-on services like hotspot or international calling packs

**Read your terms**: Look for language about "service through end of billing cycle" versus "prorated upon cancellation."

### Early Termination Fees (ETFs)

Postpaid contracts often include:
- $200-350 ETF if you leave before contract end
- Reduced by $10-15 per month served
- Sometimes waived if porting to specific carriers (Nexitel isn't typically included in waiver programs)

### Device Payment Plans

Completely separate from service charges:
- Remaining device balance becomes due immediately, OR
- Continues on payment schedule (carrier dependent)
- Interest may apply if you don't pay in full

**AT&T and T-Mobile**: Typically allow continued device payments. Verizon: Often requires full payment within 30-60 days.

## How to Minimize Port Problems

### Before You Start

1. **Screenshot everything**: Account number, PIN, billing address, IMEI, current plan details
2. **Pay your bill**: Ensure zero outstanding balance
3. **Check device unlock status**: If keeping your phone, request unlock before porting
4. **Backup your data**: Texts, voicemails, contacts stored with carrier
5. **Note your billing cycle date**: Port right after bill generation to minimize prorated charges

### During the Port

1. **Keep old SIM accessible**: Don't destroy it until port confirms complete
2. **Have both phones ready**: Old phone to verify service stops, new phone to verify activation
3. **Test during business hours**: Monday-Friday 9 AM - 5 PM ports resolve issues faster
4. **Don't factory reset your old phone**: You might need to retrieve the account number or PIN

### After Port Completion

1. **Test incoming calls from different carriers**: Ask friends on various networks to call
2. **Check voicemail setup**: Configure new Nexitel voicemail immediately
3. **Update 2FA settings**: Anywhere you use your number for authentication
4. **Monitor for final bill**: Set a calendar reminder to check for charges
