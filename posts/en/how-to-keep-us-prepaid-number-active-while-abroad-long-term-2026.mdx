## Why Keeping Your US Number Active Matters

When you're planning an extended stay abroad—whether for work, study, or family reasons—losing your US phone number can create serious problems. That number is tied to your bank accounts, two-factor authentication, job applications, tax documents, and countless online services. Letting it lapse means losing access to critical accounts and facing a frustrating reactivation process when you return.

The challenge? Most prepaid carriers require you to use your service regularly or maintain expensive plans to keep your number alive. Roaming internationally can cost $10+ per day, and many budget carriers simply deactivate numbers after 60-90 days of non-use.

## How Long Before Your Number Gets Deactivated?

Most US prepaid carriers have strict activity requirements:

- **Major carriers**: 60-90 days without activity typically triggers deactivation
- **Some budget MVNOs**: As short as 30 days in certain cases
- **"Activity" definitions**: Usually means making a call, sending a text, or using data on the US network

Once your number is deactivated, you usually have a grace period (often 30-60 days) to reactivate before it's released back into the available number pool and reassigned to someone else. After that, your number is gone permanently.

## The Hidden Costs of "Just Keeping It Active"

Many people think they can maintain their number by:

1. **Keeping an expensive plan active**: Paying $40-60/month for a plan they're not using
2. **International roaming**: Racking up $10-15/day in roaming charges whenever they check messages
3. **Auto-renewal without usage**: Paying monthly fees while abroad, only to find the carrier deactivated the line anyway for "non-use"

These approaches waste hundreds or thousands of dollars over a multi-month stay abroad.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Mistake 1: Waiting Until the Last Minute

Don't try to switch carriers the week before your flight. Port your number at least 2-3 weeks early to resolve any issues.

### Mistake 2: Forgetting to Update Payment Methods

If your US credit card expires while you're abroad, your auto-pay fails and your service lapses. Update payment info or use a card with a distant expiration date.

### Mistake 3: Not Testing SMS Delivery

Some international locations have spotty SMS delivery. Test before you rely on it for critical 2FA codes. Consider using authentication apps (Google Authenticator, Authy) as backup.

### Mistake 4: Assuming Roaming Works Everywhere

Your Nexitel plan doesn't include international roaming. Don't expect to use your US SIM card abroad for mobile data. Get a local SIM or use WiFi.

### Mistake 5: Ignoring Time Zones

If you need to call US numbers regularly, remember the time difference. Calls made during US business hours from Asia or the Middle East might mean middle-of-the-night calling for you.
